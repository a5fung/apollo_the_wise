# #368 — did the theme's other members move too?

**His question, 2026-09-13:** *"some of these stocks drives the theme because they may be one of
the leaders while the theme may drive the group, so it's a bit complicated."*

An `n` label is ambiguous. This separates the two things it can mean, at $0, from
`mi_daily_closes` and the `mi_themes` snapshots.

- group moved together → the **theme** was driving
- only this name moved → the **name** was driving (reflexivity)

Every return is **market-adjusted** — SPY's same-day move subtracted. A day when everything rose
would otherwise fake co-movement. (The repo's own `co_moving` helper is *not* market-adjusted
while the birth gate's copy is — a known doc-vs-code disagreement, filed separately.)

## Method and population

**Population:** the **44** rows of `mi_theme_relevance_cohort` with `stratum='themed'` that he
labelled on 2026-09-13 — EP alerts dated **2026-08-04 → 2026-09-08**, every one enrolled under the
**current** engine (the pre-scrap cohort is deliberately excluded; mixing them is the era error
that has bitten this repo before). n = 21 `y`, 23 `n`. No row dropped: all 44 priced.

**How each row was measured.** Theme membership = the `tickers` array from the most recent
`mi_themes` row for that theme with `theme_date <= alert_date` **and a non-empty array** (see the
defect note below — the non-empty clause is load-bearing). Peers = those members minus the alerted
ticker. For the alert date, each peer's return is `close/prev_close − 1` from `mi_daily_closes`,
**minus SPY's same-day return over the same two sessions**. Per row we keep the median peer excess
and the share of peers with positive excess; the tables below report the **median across rows**
within each label. The alerted name's own excess is computed identically, as a control.

**Window:** the alert day itself — one session. A theme that drove a group over weeks but not on
the alert day reads as "group did not follow" here.

## ⚠ But it decays exactly where there is more group to observe — say this out loud

| sample | y | n | gap (median / share) |
|---|---|---|---|
| all rows | 21 | 23 | **+4.13pp / +33.3 pts** |
| themes with ≥2 peers | 17 | 16 | +4.21pp / +50.0 pts |
| themes with ≥3 peers | 10 | 8 | +1.89pp / +18.6 pts |
| **themes with ≥5 peers** | 9 | 4 | **+0.70pp / +0.1 pts** |

**The separation is strongest where there is least to measure.** The median theme carries **2
members** — one peer — so "median peer excess" is often a single stock, and a 100%/0% share-up is
a coin flip on one or two names. On themes big enough to call a group, the effect is ~gone
(n=13, thin).

**A 2-member median is itself the finding underneath the finding.** A two-stock theme barely
supports the idea of a group moving together, which is what the whole theme axis assumes.

## ⚠ A defect in the first run of this test, recorded so it is not repeated

The first run reported a clean-looking answer on **22 of 44 rows with a median theme size of 1**.
`mi_themes` carries rows whose `tickers` array is **empty** on many dates; a naive *most recent
row ≤ alert_date* lands on one of those and reports a one-member theme with no peers. Those
numbers were discarded, not adjusted. **The tell was the diagnostic line, not the result** — the
result looked fine.

## What this does NOT answer

- **Settles:** his concern is real and it accounts for **roughly half** his no's. Pooling the 23
  as "false positives" would have been wrong.
- **Does not settle:** the theme-boost magnitude. The effect decays to nothing on themes with ≥5
  members, on n=13. **Do not set a number on this alone.**
- **Opens:** the other twelve. The group moved, the theme was real, and he still said no — that
  is a naming/membership question and it is a different fix from tightening the axis.

Cost: **$0**. Read-only; no strategy, sizing, exit or trade state touched.

Beyond the three above, this analysis does **not** answer:

- **Whether the theme boost should change at all, or by how much.** The effect is gone on themes
  with ≥5 members (n=13). This is evidence about what his labels MEAN, not a magnitude.
- **Anything about the themeless half.** 136 `themeless_winner` rows are unlabelled. The failure
  this measures is "we credited a theme that was not the driver"; the opposite failure — a real
  theme we never saw — is untouched here.
- **Whether a one-session window is the right one.** A theme that pulled a group over three weeks
  and paused on the alert day is scored here as "group did not follow". A multi-day window was not
  run and could move rows between the two buckets.
- **Whether the 12 non-reflexive no's are naming or membership.** They are one or the other; this
  does not distinguish them, and the fixes differ.
- **Anything about returns.** No forward return is used, by design — themes are not judged on
  returns until the engine is right.

# NULL CONTROL — run 2026-09-13 on his instruction

**The rule and both bars were written before the query ran** (in `nullctl.py`'s docstring), per his
steer that conclusions come from a stated rule against a stated goal.

**Rule.** For each labelled alert, compare the theme's own peers against a **matched control**: the
nearest non-theme name by 20-session realised volatility, one per peer, from the same day's priced
universe, excluding every ticker in any theme that day. Both SPY-adjusted. The theme is credited
only with the **excess of its group over that control**:

```
lift = median(theme peers' excess) − median(matched control's excess)
```

**Bar A** — lift on `y` ≥ +1.00pp AND the y−n lift gap ≥ +1.00pp → the signal survives.
**Bar B** — lift on `y` collapses under +1.00pp → what we measured was the tide, and co-movement
must not set the boost magnitude.

## Result — Bar A is met on the full sample

| his label | n | theme peers | matched control | **LIFT** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `y` | 21 | +5.01pp | **−0.12pp** | **+4.20pp** |
| `n` | 23 | +0.88pp | +0.74pp | **+0.89pp** |

On his yes rows, matched non-theme names were **flat (−0.12pp)** while the theme's peers ran
+5.01pp. That is not the tide. On his no rows the theme's peers (+0.88) barely beat the control
(+0.74) — the group move was the tide, which is exactly what a `n` should look like.

**His labels and the rule agree once the tide is removed** — worth noting given he had just
cautioned that his labels are supplementary.

## ⚠ AND THE HEADLINE DOES NOT SURVIVE ITS OWN ROBUSTNESS CHECK — the bar was met, and I am still
## saying do not use it

| sample | y lift | n lift | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥1 peer (all) | +4.20pp | +0.89pp | **+3.31pp** |
| ≥2 peers | +4.20pp | +0.74pp | +3.46pp |
| **≥3 peers** | **+0.05pp** | **+0.94pp** | **−0.89pp ← sign reverses** |
| ≥5 peers | +1.90pp | +1.39pp | +0.51pp |

At ≥3 peers the gap goes **negative**, on n=10 against n=8. The headline is carried by themes with
**one or two** peers, where a "median peer excess" is a single stock. This is not goalpost-moving:
Bar A asked *is the signal real*, and the answer is *on this sample yes, but this sample cannot
support it.*

**The blocker underneath every result in this document: the median theme has 2 members.** Until
themes are bigger, every group-level measure here is noise-dominated.
