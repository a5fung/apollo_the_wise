## 🔎 Deeper rigor (2026-07-13, post-run): the EXISTING shadows can't evidence M1-d either

Chasing Option A ("lean on the deterministic shadow") surfaced a second, code-certain gap:

- **M1-d's boost only *changes* a MODERATE alert.** `compose_final_tier` moves the TIER
  (none→MODERATE→HIGH). A +1 on a HIGH alert **clamps to HIGH — a no-op**. So M1-d only
  actually acts by promoting a **MODERATE→HIGH** (a hot-theme name that fires an alert it
  wouldn't have) — that is the consequential population.
- **The theme shadows only ever logged HIGH alerts.** `log_theme_axis_shadow` +
  `log_theme_axis_adjusted_shadow` both sit under `if r.get("score_tier") == "HIGH":`
  (ep_detector.py:3254). So all 461 `mi_theme_axis_shadow` rows are HIGH — the tier where the
  boost does nothing — and the credit-boost log (`theme_axis_shadow_adjusted`) has **1 row**.
- **Net: no data — free OR paid — observes what M1-d actually does.** M1-b tested the wrong
  MECHANISM (judge-prompt vs deterministic compose); the shadows watched the wrong TIER (HIGH,
  where the boost clamps). The MODERATE→HIGH promotions M1-d would cause were never logged.

**Consequence for 7/18:** M1-d is **not signable on evidence** — there is no evidence on the
population it changes.

### Empirical sizing (prod, 2026-07-13) — sparse, and NOT reliably measurable yet

The would-boost population is MODERATE alerts (where +1 → HIGH is a real change). Over the full
recorded history (all `mi_ep_alerts`, 2026-04-13 → 07-08): **83 MODERATE alerts; 63 settled.**

⚠️ **The MODERATE theme-membership count is genuinely uncertain — the two available signals both
undercount and disagree ~3×:**
- The stored `in_active_theme` flag shows just **1/83 MODERATE** (and 20/479 HIGH = 4.2%) — but a
  cross-tab against the shadow's `get_theme_membership` stage proves the flag is **unreliable**:
  of 64 shadow-themed HIGH alerts, the flag said "true" only 20× (18 flat false, 26 NULL). It's a
  strict UNDERcount (never false-positive, frequently missing). So "1/83" is a floor, not the truth.
- The reliable signal (`get_theme_membership`, ~**12%** of HIGH themed) was **only ever computed
  for HIGH alerts** — the shadow's HIGH-only gate means MODERATE theme membership was never
  measured with the good signal at all.

**What is robust despite the uncertainty:** theme membership among alerts is low (4–12%) and
rarer for MODERATE, so M1-d's boostable population is a *handful per quarter at most* — still too
sparse to reach any decision-N on a near horizon (backfill and forward-accrual are both weak), and
still a small live footprint.

**Two problems, and the deeper one is upstream:**
1. M1-d (the credit table) can't be evidenced or even sized until (2) is fixed.
2. **The theme-MEMBERSHIP layer is inconsistent + undercounts** (two signals disagree 3×; the
   good one is HIGH-only; the stored flag is NULL ~31% of the time). This is the known #319/#325
   coverage-blind-spot, and it sits UPSTREAM of the credit math M1-d tunes.

**So M1-d is a keep/shelve question (operator's, THE LINE), but the honest headline is: the
theme-axis PROGRAM is blocked on membership-signal quality, not on the credit table.** The wire-in
stays safely dark regardless. Building (a)/(b) to evidence M1-d is premature while the membership
signal underneath is untrustworthy.
