## The order-structure decision

**Gemini's instruction was: split the entry into two orders** — 1/3 with a +2R target, 2/3 without.
**That cannot be recorded in this system.** `mi_live_trades` carries
`UNIQUE (ticker, alert_date)` (index `mi_live_trades_ticker_alert_date_key`, verified against prod),
so two entry rows for one ticker on one day are impossible without a schema migration. And if they
were possible, `count_open_positions` counts rows in `OPEN_POSITION_STATUSES`, so a single ticker
would consume **two of the five** position slots — silently tightening the cap.

Gemini's own text is also self-contradictory here: it says Alpaca reduces the linked stop
automatically so we need not patch quantities, then says we must split to avoid a quantity mismatch.
If the first is true the split is unnecessary.

### Proposed design instead — reuse the proven partial machinery

**One entry, one bracket (unchanged). After the entry fills, place a resting LIMIT SELL for 1/3 at
`entry + 2 × risk_per_share`. On its fill, reduce the stop to the remaining 2/3 and move it to
breakeven.**

Why this and not a bracket take-profit leg:
- It reuses `finalize_partial_exit()` (`order_manager.py:2191`), which already commits partial state
  on the real WebSocket fill, under the per-trade advisory lock, idempotent on `order_id`.
- It leaves the entry path, the position cap, the dedup key and #500's price-aware entry untouched.
- One trade row, one position slot. No migration.

⚠ **Confirmed NOT paused**: `_PARTIAL_EXIT_PAUSED = False` (`order_manager.py:1398`). The #151 hard
pause was lifted; the machinery is live. (Its docstring still says "PAUSED" — stale comment, worth
fixing in this build.)

### The one real unknown — the over-covered stop window

`execute_partial_exit` deliberately **reduces the stop FIRST, then sells**, holding a DB advisory lock
across the whole sequence, so the position is never unprotected and the reconciler cannot fight it.

**A resting limit inverts that**: the broker fills the sell *first*, and only then do we learn about
it (WebSocket) and replace the stop. Between those two events the stop covers **more shares than are
held**.

**This is the thing to answer before writing code, and it cannot be answered from the repo:**
1. Does Alpaca reject, auto-reduce, or leave an over-covering stop after a partial position
   reduction?
2. If it leaves it, what happens if that stop then triggers — does it try to sell shares we do not
   have, and does that reject the whole order?

**How to answer it: a paper smoke test, before any live code.** Place a bracket, fill it, rest a
limit for 1/3, let it fill, and observe the stop leg's state. That is ~20 minutes and it decides the
design. `alpaca_client.replace_order()` (`:447`) exists for the follow-up replace.

## Pre-committed reversion triggers (from the signed proposal)

1. Partial fires, remainder stopped at breakeven, trade then runs ≥+4R same session — one occurrence
   is a review, two is a revert.
2. Next 10 closed live trades worse than the same 10 replayed under the incumbent.
3. Reversion is a config flag for the *decision*; note that resting orders on OPEN positions must
   also be cancelled, so plan the revert as an operation.

## What this build is NOT expected to do

**zero winners across bull AND correcting months**. This reduces the size of losses. The upstream
question (why live trades die in 1.5 days) is #503 and is untouched by this.
