SUMMARY — what the two parts say, and the fork (nothing pre-decided; grading is his)
==================================================================================================
PART 1 · The expectedness axis is DERIVABLE from what we already store (live corpus: 86%
  on scheduled/unscheduled, 75% on backward/forward, deterministic, $0) and every
  measurable comparison runs in the operator's predicted direction:
  - unscheduled 11.6% reach >=8xADR vs scheduled 3.8% (P90 8.7x vs 5.3x; p=0.20/0.13)
  - pure forward-changing 13.9% + P90 10.4x vs backward 0 of 14
  - gap-controlled: the unscheduled/forward side leads in ALL SIX tercile cells (6-of-6
    sign consistency; every cell individually below the house N floors)
  - the MEDIANS run the other way (scheduled 2.7x vs unscheduled 1.8x): the unexpected
    catalyst under-delivers typically and over-delivers in the tail — the exact fat-tail
    signature this program is hunting, and the same shape as the extension-filter cohort.
  VERDICT: a CANDIDATE, not a finding — 96 classifiable full-window alerts hold ~7 tail
  winners; no cell can clear the permutation floors at this N. NOT a null: direction is
  uniform. It accrues for free as alerts settle (and would have been testable at 3x the N
  had the purge not eaten March/April).
PART 2 · The three-line rule (small gap + tight EP day + low MA extension, percentile-
  averaged) ranks the 26 tradeable >=10R winners far above the candidate pool:
  - top decile catches 7 of 26 (27%) at 2.7x lift; top quartile 16 of 26 (62%) at 2.5x;
    median winner percentile 20th of 732
  - take-the-day's-top-1 catches a >=10R winner on 5 of the 10 winner sessions
