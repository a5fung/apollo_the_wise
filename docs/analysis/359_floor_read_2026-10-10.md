# #359 — is $500M the right market-cap floor for EP entries? (read of 2026-10-10)

**Answer: the data cannot tell the two sides of the floor apart.** Names just under $500M produce big winners
(≥ 3R) about as often as names just over it, on both populations below. The one consistent difference is
liquidity: the names under the floor trade about half the dollars by the open. Research only. The floor is an
entry criterion, so any change is his decision (THE LINE).

## Method and population

Two $0 reads from stored data. Both are walked under the **current MAGNA53 bracket (era D)**: stop at entry − 2R,
one third sold at +8 ORB-R, stop moved to entry at +3 ORB-R, trail on max(SMA10, SMA20). R = money-R. "Tail" means
a settled trade at ≥ 3R (and ≥ 8R).

**A — same rule on both sides of the floor, 2024-01-02 → 2026-09-03** (`scripts/probes/_359_floor/bothsides_walk.py`, `bothsides_stats.py` → `bothsides_stats_out.txt`).
- Re-ran the #624 backfill screen with its $500M cap ceiling **lifted**. Every name that met the lane rule (gap ≥ 15%
  at a post-open tick, volume ≥ 90th percentile, prior close ≥ $5) became a signal, whatever its cap. All went
  through the identical walker (`walk624.walk_primary`) on the captured #624 files.
- **Population: 1,335 signals.** 768 are under $500M, the same 768 as #624's population, which reproduces exactly:
  317 walked, 10 at ≥ 3R. The other 567 are at or above $500M.
- Cap = Polygon point-in-time shares × raw prior close. #624's calibration C3 found it puts a name on the same side
  of $500M as the live cap 97.7% of the time. 89 signals sit at $400–600M, where side assignment is least certain.
- **Q** = the signals that also pass the live quality stack: ADV$ ≥ $1M, ATR ≤ 15%, not extended, pre-market shares
  ≥ 25k. Q is the lane-rule population after the live quality stack. A lower floor under the real EP rules would also admit
  gaps of 9–15%, which A does not cover; read B is the real-rule view.
- The safeguards (breaker, position cap) are not modelled, so names they blocked are in.
- Deviation: `lib624.assert_frozen()` was not called. `ep_detector.py` has changed since 10-06 (#694 shortlist
  ranking and audit thresholds). The five constants lib624 takes from it were re-asserted and are unchanged.

**B — the real EP scan population, 2026-06-08 → 2026-09-03** (`scanlog_stats.py` → `scanlog_stats_out.txt`).
- Population: `_623_master.jsonl`, every `mi_ep_scan_log` ticker-day, filtered and safeguard-blocked rows included.
  3,431 of its 3,458 rows join to the era-D walk in `_545p2_623_era_walk.tsv` (cell `era_d_ladder`, submit 09:31).
- The comparison is stage-matched.
  - **Under the floor:** rejected by `mcap_too_small`. Rejection order is ADV → ATR → cap, so these names had
    already passed the universe floors, shortlist, rvol, cooldown, extension, ADV$ and ATR.
  - **Over the floor:** cleared `check_filters`, i.e. reached scoring or was rejected only after grading.
- Under-floor caps come from the live value in the filter text (79 of 82). Over-floor caps are 85 of 91 proxy
  (current shares × prior close; #623 measured a 3.5% median error).
- Caveat: this window is the in-sample window where the low-cap thresholds were chosen. #624 found tail rates there
  about 3× history. Both bands share the window, so the comparison between them is fair, but the levels are inflated.

## What it says

- **Big winners:** the same rate on both sides. A: 3 of 38 vs 3 of 39. B: 7 of 59 vs 5 of 62. Every interval
  overlaps almost completely. Lowering the floor would add winners and losers in about the same proportion.
- **Average:** on the long history (A) the admitted band is better, by about 0.35R a trade. On the hot window (B)
  the blocked band looks better, but only because of one near-zero-stop trade. Neither difference is outside noise.
- **Liquidity:** consistently worse below the floor. Median ADV$ is $3.3M vs $7.8M, and dollars traded by the open
  are about half. The two reads measure early volume differently (their levels differ ~20×), but the ratio holds in both. Live spreads on the blocked names run 11–556 bps, median about 200. This is the only axis that separates the bands,
  and it favours keeping the floor.
- **Below $250M is worse, not a hidden opportunity:** $100–250M is 0 of 68 at ≥ 3R across all signals (A). BCAR
  ($227M, the 08-18 seed case) does not appear in either population.

## What this does not answer

- **How many trades a real answer needs.** The #624 formula, 24.4 × (1 − p) ÷ p, gives about 280 walked trades
  per band at an ~8% tail rate before a band's rate is pinned to a 2× interval. History holds about 38 per band. The
  live lane walks roughly 3 per month at $250–500M. **No accrual horizon in reach separates these two bands.**
- **Fill quality.** Neither route models slippage or spread cost, and above-floor spreads are not recorded.
- **Whether the EP score bar would admit the blocked names.** Under-floor names are never scored, so A compares on
  the lane rule and B at the stage before scoring. Neither reaches "would this have been a HIGH alert".
- **Book effects.** Safeguards, slots, sizing and the daily loss limit are not modelled.
- **Any exit other than era D** (#545 owns exits).
- **Single-trade 3R calls are soft.** The two walkers price the seed trade BLZE 06-23 about 1R apart: +3.93R in A
  (Polygon day-0 bars, tick-timed submit) and +2.93R in B (stored bars, 09:31 submit). It is a tail event in one
  table and not the other. The band-level comparison does not move.
- **How this relates to the PLAN line's 09-04 "n = 7 blocked by the floor alone, +1.76R".** That came from the
  #622 study under the retired +2R exit, on a narrower near-miss slice. It is not re-derived here. B's
  stage-matched band (82 rows, 59 settled at $250–500M) is the era-D replacement for it.

## ⚖ THE LINE

- The floor (`MIN_MARKET_CAP`, `backtester/filters.py:23`) is an entry criterion. Nothing here changes it or
  recommends a new number.
- Options for him: keep $500M; change it (CHANGE_PROCESS, a backtest and his sign-off); or stop re-reading it on a
  timer.
