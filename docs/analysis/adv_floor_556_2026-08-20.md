# #556 — Does the $1M average-dollar-volume floor cost us? (2026-08-20)

**MEASUREMENT ONLY. No criterion changed. Any change to the floor is CHANGE_PROCESS +
operator sign-off (THE LINE).**

## The question

The 08-09 weekly digest reported `Avg volume too low` as the highest-yield skip reason on the
board: 9 names skipped in 30 days, 7 of 9 ran >=10%, average peak +30%, best +111%. The
operator asked whether the floor is protecting a risk that does not exist at a $760 live
position. Live positions average **$760** (max $989); paper averages **$15,399**.

## Data

- Source: `mi_ep_missed_outcomes`, `skip_category='adv_low'`, 2026-04-14 -> 2026-08-19.
- 241 rows / 167 distinct names. Split by reason:
  - **214 rows / 145 names** `filter:adv_too_low` (the ADV value is embedded in `skip_reason`).
  - **26 rows** `filter:adv_no_data` — a DATA-COVERAGE failure, not a liquidity judgement.
    Kept out of every number below; see "Separate finding".
- Return basis is **`open_d0`, the gap-day open** (`missed_outcomes.py:480`) — "what a day-2
  chaser pays". It is NOT our ORB-high entry, so every return here is more generous than a
  real fill on a name that worked, and more punishing on one that faded (we would not have
  been filled at all — the stop-buy never triggers).
- Capture: `scratchpad/556_adv_low.psv`, pulled once, read many (cost rule).

## Result 1 — the digest's read does not survive a 4-month window

| | 9-name digest slice (30d) | This study (145 names, 4 months) |
|---|---|---|
| ran >=10% | 7 of 9 (78%) | 19% at 5d |
| median outcome | not stated | **-2.1% at 5d, -4.5% at 20d** |
| dropped >=10% | not stated | 30% at 5d |

The 30-day slice was too small and favourably drawn. **On the median, the floor is keeping us
out of names that go down.** N=9 is below the CHANGE_PROCESS N>=10 bar; this study is not.

## Result 2 — but there IS a real tail, and unusually it HOLDS (P3)

A median cannot see what we hunt. Tail, measured from the gap-day open:

| ADV band | n | peak5 >=30% | peak20 >=50% | peak20 >=100% | median 20d close |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <$250k | 63 | 25% | 22% | **16%** | -4.8% |
| $250-500k | 61 | 20% | 11% | 5% | -6.2% |
| $500-750k | 44 | 20% | 23% | 2% | -6.6% |
| $750k-1M | 46 | 26% | 13% | 2% | +0.3% |
| **ALL** | **214** | **23%** | **17%** | **7%** | **-4.5%** |

**28 distinct names (19% of the cohort) reached a >=50% 20-day peak.** Biggest: VEEE +847%,
JLHL +401%, AMIX +372%, HCAI +338%, NXTS +330%.

⚠ **A number NOT to over-read.** Those 28 names close 20d at a median **+28%** against their
+86% median peak — but they were SELECTED BY their peak, so a positive close is close to
built in. It says the runs were not one-day spikes; it is **not** evidence that admitting the
cohort is +EV. **The unconditional number is the one that governs: median 20-day close
-4.5% across all 145 names.**

The >=100% gradient is real, not a repeat-name artifact — recomputed on DISTINCT names (best
peak per name) it holds at **16% / 5% / 4% / 0%** as ADV rises. The >=50% gradient does NOT
(24/12/28/14) and should not be cited.

Caveat: 37 tail rows across 28 names — JLHL appears 3x, HCAI/TRT/BGDE/NXTS 2x each. Some of
the tail is the same name re-alerting.

## Result 3 — "loosen it a bit" is the worst available move

The obvious proposal is to drop the floor from $1M to ~$500k. That band is the **dead zone**:

- $500k-$1M: 63 names, **only 2% reached a >=100% peak**, median 20-day close **-2.5%**.
- The >=100% tail is monotone in the other direction: 16% / 5% / 2% / 2% as ADV rises.

**Halving the floor buys the weakest slice.** This finding does not rest on the tail
statistics at all — it is a direct measurement of the exact band such a move would admit.
(Median ADV of the tail cohort, $366,616, is NOT meaningfully different from the rest,
$421,957 — that comparison carries nothing and is not used.)

## Result 4 — at live size the tail is tradeable; at paper size it is not

Order as a share of one day's dollar volume, across the 28 tail names:

| Size | median | worst |
|---|---|---|
| live $760 | **0.21%** | 3.6% |
| live max $989 | 0.27% | 4.7% |
| paper $15,399 | **4.20%** | **73.3%** |

The operator's premise is confirmed on the entry: **$760 is not a liquidity constraint even in
a $60k/day name.** At paper size the same names are untradeable. The floor's justification is
genuinely size-dependent and nobody re-derived it when live size shrank.

⚠ This is the ENTRY. A resting stop-loss in a $200k/day name is a different exposure and is
not measured here.

## Result 5 (decisive) — ADV is gate #1, so loosening it alone changes almost nothing

`backtester/filters.py::check_filters` runs **ADV -> ATR% -> market cap, in that order**. Every
one of these 145 names failed at gate 1 and was **never tested against ATR% or the $500M market
cap floor**. So the prize is not 145 names.

Of the 145, `mi_market_caps` covers 32; **4 clear $500M (12.5%)**. None appear in
`mi_stock_scores` at all. A sub-$1M/day name with a $500M+ market cap would need <0.2% daily
turnover — rare. ⚠ **That 32-name subsample is BIASED and the bias runs one way:** these names
are cached only because some OTHER lane looked them up — they never reached the mcap gate here
— which skews toward larger, better-known names. The true clear rate is therefore likely
BELOW 12.5%, which makes the no-op conclusion stronger, not weaker. Extrapolated, loosening
ADV alone admits roughly **17 names over 4 months**,
which then still face ATR%, RVOL, score >=50, catalyst quality, top-20 and extension.

**Live confirmation the same day:** the 9% gap floor admitted 6 names on 2026-08-20 and
downstream killed all 6 — two of them (HVT $441M, OPHC $127M) on this exact market-cap floor.

## ⚠ What this study does NOT answer

**No R-per-trade was computed.** Every number here is a raw excursion from the gap-day open,
not a result under our entry and stop. So **"should we loosen the floor" is not yet
answerable** — this study establishes what the floor is and is not costing, and that the
lever is coupled to the market-cap floor. The +EV question needs a fill model (see the DoD
note below).

## What this means

1. **The floor is NOT costing us where anyone would loosen it to.** $500k-$1M is the dead zone.
2. **The tail that matters sits at $60k-$370k/day**, and at $760 we could genuinely trade it.
3. **ADV and market cap are near-redundant gates and must be decided TOGETHER.** Moving ADV
   alone is close to a no-op; moving it without the mcap floor is measuring the wrong lever.
   This folds into **#359** (the market-cap floor fork) and **#577** (price every gate that
   kills a name before evaluation).
4. **Any loosening needs a re-tighten trigger tied to position size** — at paper scale these
   names are untradeable, so the floor must come back up before live size approaches it.

## DoD item not delivered, and why

DoD (3) asked for **realistic ORB fills modelled at $760 and $15,399**. Delivered: the
liquidity share above, which answers tradeable-vs-not. **Not delivered: a simulated ORB entry
and exit**, because that needs intraday bars for these micro-caps and the #562 backfill does
not cover them. The share-of-volume figures are the honest substitute; a fill model would be
the next step **only if** the operator wants to move the ADV and market-cap floors together —
on the numbers above, moving ADV alone does not clear the bar before slippage is even reached.

## Separate finding — `adv_no_data` (26 rows)

26 rows were killed not for low volume but because `mi_daily_closes` held **fewer than 10 bars
in the prior 30 days** for that ticker (`_check_adv_dollar_volume` returns `FILTER_ADV_NO_DATA`
when the `HAVING COUNT(*) >= 10` clause fails). That is a data-coverage defect, not a judgement
about the name. Under **P1** it is the worse exclusion: the name was never evaluated at all,
and leaves no trace beyond this row. Not averaged into anything above. Tracked under **#577**.
