## H10 — stops scored in capped dollars, not equal-risk R: HOLDS (the ranking changes)

Dollar result = R × min(regime multiplier, 0.20 × stop width ÷ RISK_PCT), at RISK_PCT 1% (pre-10-05) and 2% (now).
The lane's own stop (median 2.9% wide in ERA A, ~1.6% in ERA B) is capped on 66-90% of fires.

- **ERA B (pooled, lane exit): every wider stop that beats the lane's stop in R LOSES to it in dollars**, at 1% and
  2% (1× ADR: +2.6R but −5.4 / −4.1 dollar-R; 1.5× ADR +5.6R but −4.1 / −4.3). No ERA B fire reaches 3R on any
  stop, so the tight capped stop simply loses fewer dollars. Inside single patterns a few wider stops win on both
  (6-23 pairs — direction only).
- **ERA A:** the wider stops keep more of the big winners' dollars (1× ADR: 38 fires ≥ 3R worth +171.9 dollar-R at
  1% vs the lane stop's 34 worth +135.4), but on the head-to-head the dollar edge turns negative once the two best
  names are dropped — **except the low-reclaim 1× ADR stop** (the candidate earlier docs carried; #616 reads it
  forward): +32.6R, +2.5 dollar-R at 1%, +2.9 at 2% after drop-best-two (171 pairs; ~80% of the edge is BHVN, BLSH,
  EFOR), and it FLIPS in ERA B (7 pairs). [verifier correction — the first draft said no ERA A stop survived]
- At today's 2%, the re-entry study's ERA A 1× ADR trail cell flips too (+94.2R, −4.2 dollar-R).
- Cannot see: ERA B is 16 campaigns; share rounding and the zero-share reject (small account) are ignored;
  breakers, position caps and slippage are not modelled; 337 fires predate the 07-26 regime-multiplier sizing.

**Meaning:** any stop proposal for the lane must be scored in capped dollars beside R — on today's sizing the
wide-stop advantage mostly disappears, and in ERA B it reverses.
