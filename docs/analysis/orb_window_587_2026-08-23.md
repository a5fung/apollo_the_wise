# #587 — The 9:45 ORB cutoff: what the 25 discarded alerts did (2026-08-23)

**MEASUREMENT ONLY. Nothing changed. The submission window is entry discipline — the operator's
sole authority (THE LINE). Every "would have" below is a fact about the tape, not a
recommendation; whether the window moves is his fork alone.**

## The question

25 HIGH alerts in the last 60 days were rejected `window:out_of_orb` for being detected
09:45–09:59 ET. Were those names worse than, the same as, or better than the ones we entered —
and could they even have been traded at the moment we saw them?

## Result 2 — the discarded class is BETTER than what we entered, and the edge is broad, not two lottery tickets

Same 60 days, same forward-return basis (d0 open):

| | rejected 25 (settled n=23) | entered 25 (settled n=22) |
|---|---|---|
| 5-day close above the gap-day open | **16 of 23 (70%)** | 7 of 22 (32%) |
| median 5-day return | **+8.6%** | −5.3% |
| median 5-day peak | **+14.1%** | +9.3% |
| touched +20% within 5 days | **9 of 25** | 4 of 25 |
| biggest 20-day peaks | +78, +40, +40, +35, +32% | +52, +46, +39, +38, +26% |

- The tail has 2 converters (BLZE, TSAT) — but underneath them 16 of 23 names closed 5 days
  GREEN. The entered book, measured identically, is 7 of 22. This is a broad quality gap, not
  two flukes on a pile of duds.
- The realized entered book over the same window: **22 closed, 2 winners, −11.3R** — itself
  carried by one name (PLTR +3.3R).
- One name is in both columns: **BLZE** — rejected 07-31 (the +78% run), then entered on a
  fresh 08-04 alert and **lost** −1.0R. We caught the name and missed the move.
- Why the gap is plausible, not a data fluke: all 25 entries came from PRE-OPEN detections
  (gap-at-open names, which often fade); a 9:45+ detection is a name still making highs 25
  minutes into the session — the tape has already confirmed it. The window keeps the
  confirmed class and trades only the unconfirmed one.

## Result 3 — could they have been traded? Mostly yes, at a small premium

Checked on the 20 with minute bars (ORB = the 9:30 bar, verified against stored ORB values):

- At the detection minute the median name sat **+2.1% above its ORB high** — 16 of 20 above.
  So the clean ORB-high breakout price was gone, but barely.
- The live pipeline already has a path for exactly this shape (#500 limit-chase, capped at
  1.5× planned risk): **10 of 20 pass that cap as-is** under the current 2R stop. 13 of 20
  came back to the ORB high later the same day (a resting stop-limit would have filled).
- **The "trade was never available" objection kills only AMRC** (opened at its high, was
  −8.4% below the ORB high by 9:45, no valid geometry — also the cohort's worst name; the
  window did its job exactly once).
- Cost of lateness: by detection the median name had already moved ~+4% off the open — the
  Result-2 table overstates what a late entry captures by about that much. The simulation
  below uses the actual detection price, so it does not inherit this flattery.

Simulated late entry — detection-minute close ×1.002, the CURRENT 2R stop, stop checked
before target inside every bar (pessimistic), no partials, mark at day-5 close (19 names;
5 lack bars, AMRC untradeable):

- **+10.4R total**: 6 stop-outs, 3 reached the +2R partial target, BLZE +5.7R and TSAT +3.5R.
- Without those two: **+1.2R across 17** — breakeven-plus. The class pays like EPs pay:
  small losses, small scratches, occasional multiple-R winners. At the ~$50 Bull risk budget,
  +10.4R ≈ +$500 over 60 days — versus the −11.3R (≈ −$550) the entered book realized.
- The 5 un-simulated names (PENG/IREN/HAS/NNE/HGTY) all peaked +10% to +35% — their absence
  more likely understates than overstates the total.

## Result 4 — era-scope: the signal is pre-rebuild; the current-era N is 2

- **2R-stop era (08-18 →)**: 2 of the 25 (TWST, UUUU) and 3 entries. Nothing decidable.
- **Selection-rebuild era (separation scoring + lattice + shortlist, operator-signed 08-22,
  deploying in the 08-23 window)**: **zero** of the 25 fired under it. 7 of the 25 are
  routine-graded and 15 scored 60.5 or below on the legacy scale — under the new bar an
  unknown, possibly large, fraction would not alert at all. The forward population of this class cannot be inferred from these 25.
- So the honest claim is: **under the OLD selection, the cutoff discarded a better-than-entered
  class for 60 straight days.** Whether the new selection still routes its best post-open
  names into the same bin is exactly what the next weeks of `window:out_of_orb` rows will show
  — the skip rows and missed-outcomes rows are already being recorded; no new telemetry needed.

## Result 5 — the mechanism: a one-incident rule, and a scanner that cannot see before 9:45

- The window (9:31–9:44) was tightened from 10:00 on **2026-04-24** (commit `30cf961e`) off a
  single case: CHE first fired HIGH at 9:55 after the tape had faded; the argument was "a HIGH
  at 9:55 against a 9:30–9:31 ORB is a stale setup by construction," and the 9:45 number was
  chosen to "match the 15-min gate already used for EP projection." **No backtest, no N — an
  incident fix plus a symmetry argument.** The SSoT change log records no evidence for the
  cutoff level then or since.
- The scan runs every 5 minutes (observed scoring latency up to ~3 min), so the last tick
  that can beat the cutoff is ~9:40. Four of the 25 were stamped 09:45 — one tick late.
- The deeper interlock: in these 60 days the detector produced **84 pre-open HIGHs, ONE in
  9:31–9:44 (MPWR 07-31 — then eaten by the circuit-breaker cooldown), and 26 at 9:45+.**
  Post-open confirmation (session RVOL, the ≥9:45 projection gate) structurally cannot fire
  much before 9:45. The practical effect of the current geometry is not "we trim stragglers" —
  it is **the entire post-open detection lane lands one tick after the door shuts**, ~96% of it
  discarded, 0 of it traded in 60 days.

## ⚠ What this does NOT answer

- **What the current system would do with this class** — 0 of the 25 fired under the rebuilt
  selection; the 2R-era overlap is n=2. The measured edge belongs to the old-selection era.
- **What live fills would have been** — the sim assumes an instant fill at detection close
  ×1.002, no partial-taking, no trailing, no slippage, hold to day-5 close. The live exit
  ladder would shape every one of these numbers (both directions: partials trim BLZE, the
  trail could also exit it far higher).
- **Tradability for 5 of the 25** — no minute bars; their outcomes are in every cohort stat
  but not in the chase/sim numbers.
- **Whether 9:45 is the right cutoff, or what a different one should be** — that is entry
  discipline, the operator's sole authority. This doc establishes only what the current
  cutoff discarded and that its level was never evidence-set.
- **Nothing here touched any window, threshold, config, or trade state.**

## What this means

1. **Yes — over these 60 days the cutoff threw away the better half of the alert stream**:
   70% of discarded names were green 5 days later vs 32% of entered ones, and 2 of the
   period's 4 biggest would-be winners (BLZE +78%, TSAT +38%) died in this bin while the
   entered book realized −11.3R.
2. **The premise check passes**: most of the 25 were tradeable at detection at a ~2-4%
   premium; half already fit the existing chase-cap rule; only AMRC was truly gone.
3. **The rule was never evidence-based**: a 2026-04-24 single-incident fix (CHE) whose 9:45
   level was an analogy, not a measurement — and the 5-min scan cadence makes it discard the
   whole post-open lane, not just stale stragglers.
4. **The era caveat is real**: the current selection rebuild has produced 0 members of this
   class so far; the existing skip + missed-outcomes telemetry will show within weeks whether
   the new selection still fills this bin. **Whether and how the window moves is the
   operator's fork; no level is proposed here.**
