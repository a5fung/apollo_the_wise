## What the tops have in common (synthesis — mine; his source words above are the authority)

| signal | seen on | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Price cuts through BOTH the 21-day and 50-day on huge / increasing volume** | ZM, PTON, DOCU, PDD, FVRR, ROKU, DKNG | Boik's *"classic sell signal"* — the trend has changed |
| **The broken averages then act as resistance** | ZM, PTON, DOCU, PDD, DKNG | the confirmation: rallies stall at the 21/50 instead of bouncing off them |
| **21-day crosses under the 50-day** (and the reverse starts a new uptrend) | GRWG, DKNG; PDD, FSLY, DKNG (reverse) | a slower, cleaner trend-state switch |
| **Climax action**: a vertical run (doubling in weeks), a huge gap-up, then a reversal on massive volume | DOCU, DKNG, FSLY, PTON | the run's end, usually before the averages break |
| **Volume dries up or flips**: *"volume slows down near the top"*, *"no volume buying power"*, *"selling volume dwarfs buying volume"* | DOCU, ZM, PTON, NIO | demand leaving |
| **Chop**: whipsaw, *"up, over, and under key moving averages"*, *"fights its moving averages"* | ROKU, PINS, NIO, DOCU | no conviction — the leader has stopped leading |
| **Failed breakout / failed rally**: a record-volume gap-up that fails on a gap-down; rallies that *"can't make new highs"* | PINS, DKNG, NIO | lower highs |
| **Sector rotation** | DKNG | the group leaves, and the stock goes with it |

**⚠ The counter-example that makes this a judgement, not a rule — FVRR 2020:** *"Scary break of
21-day area on huge volume but recovers quickly. Add-on opportunity"* and *"Big break of 21-day and
50-day area on huge volume but recovers quickly. Add-on opportunity."* The same break on the same
volume was an ADD in 2020 and a SELL in 2021. What separated them on the chart is what happened
next — a quick reclaim versus the averages turning into resistance. Any exit rule built from this
set has to measure the reclaim, not just the break.

**How it pairs with the TraderLion set.** TraderLion teaches the entry and the add (proper base,
pocket pivot, shakeout to the respected average); Boik shows where the same leaders ended. Together
they are the entry-and-exit example set for Saturday's Block 5 and the chart-vision work (#519).

## What this does not answer
- **Point-in-time.** Every box was written knowing the ending; *"quickly fails"* and *"recovers quickly"* were not knowable on the day of the break.
- **Base rates.** Eleven famous round trips; how often the same break is a shakeout that recovers is exactly the FVRR question and is not measured here.
- **Our bars.** 2020–21 history is not in `mi_daily_closes`.
