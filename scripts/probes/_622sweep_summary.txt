#622 REDONE — "how do we trade CHPT successfully, how do we let it in?"

READ-ONLY measurement. No threshold, filter, rubric weight, or config changed
in production. Everything below is built by importing the REAL
`ep_detector._score_ep` and `ep_rubric` weight tables and feeding them
alternate inputs / alternate weight dicts — never reimplemented, never live.

============================== HEADLINE ==============================

Fixing the volume bug is not enough, and there is no honest variant found
that both admits CHPT and is genuinely profitable on this cohort.

  - Honest volume conviction alone moves CHPT from 52.5 to 58.8 (current
    rubric, bar 65) — real, but 6.2 points short. Volume was never the whole
    story.
  - The ONE feature in this data that reliably predicts outcome — market cap,
    even within this already-sub-$500M population — says CHPT (12th
    percentile of this cohort, $134M) sits in the WORST-performing third:
    bottom tercile ($84M-$222M) averages -0.56R, top tercile ($351M-$500M)
    averages +0.78R. Sign holds in both halves of the 9-week sample (H1 rho
    +0.47, H2 rho +0.18 — weaker but same direction). The operator's "maybe
    smaller is reversed" hypothesis is tested directly and does NOT hold —
    smaller is worse here too, same direction as everywhere else.
  - Under the CURRENT rubric, the only real path to the 65 bar for this
    cohort is the "gap>=10% + game_changer catalyst" conviction floor — a
    2-win/4-loss lottery here (FBRX +10.71R, SENS +3.99R won; RGNX, AGPU,
    PESI, DUOT each lost ~-1R), 33% win rate. Every variant that DOES admit
    CHPT (legacy gap ladder, doubled volume weight, rescaled liquidity)
    shows a positive average return only because it ALSO admits FBRX and/or
    SENS — the same two names every time, regardless of which lever moves.
    Remove them and every variant's average return falls to at-or-below the
    cohort baseline. FBRX and SENS are ALSO both stale-tick rows (their
    SCORE inputs came from a tick more than 5 minutes from 09:31, not their
    outcomes — realized R is unaffected) — so every variant's headline mean
    rests on two names whose admitting SCORE was not built from a clean
    09:31 read. Restricted to the fresh-tick-only subsample, every admitting
    variant is flat-to-negative (see "ROBUSTNESS" below).
  - CHPT's own result in the replay (+0.33R) is the SINGLE MOST COMMON
    outcome in this cohort — 29% of the 107 settled trades (31 names) land
    in the same narrow band (+0.28R to +0.42R), the mechanical signature of
    "hit the first partial target, then the remainder got stopped at
    breakeven." CHPT is the modal outcome, not a hidden winner and not
    especially unlucky either — the bracket did what it does to about a
    third of this cohort's trades.

============================== PART 1 — HONEST INPUTS ==============================

Three problems found in the prior study, all now fixed:

1. `vol_percentile` hardcoded to 50.0 for all 48 sampled names (the bug the
   operator flagged). Fixed: `_volume_percentile()` (imported unmodified) fed
   a rolling 20-day-average-volume history built from `mi_daily_closes`,
   evaluated strictly before each scan date — CHPT's honest value is 100.0
   (its 09:31 volume of 969,501 shares already exceeded every one of its own
   trailing 20-day-average readings from the last 60 days).

   SEPARATE, PREVIOUSLY UNDISCOVERED FINDING, two stacked causes: at 09:31
   the LIVE wrapper (`ep_detector.py` ~3844: `if _minutes_since_open is None:
   vol_pct = _volume_percentile(...) else: vol_pct = 50.0`) hardcodes 50.0
   for EVERY name at EVERY post-open tick, by design — the function is never
   even called with real data at the moment this whole question is about,
   for ANY ticker, regardless of data availability. That is the dominant,
   universal cause and it is not a bug — CLAUDE.md documents it as
   deliberate ("partial-day cumulative would falsely rank near 0 against
   full-day distributions"). Separately, and moot at 09:31 specifically but
   relevant to the PRE-market ticks where the wrapper WOULD call the real
   function: `mi_stock_scores.adv_20` (the live history source, top ~2,400
   RS-ranked names only) has ZERO rows in the 60 days before scan_date for
   33 of the 154 ticker-days in this population (21%) — CHPT itself is one
   of them (its `mi_stock_scores` coverage stops 2026-06-15, ~80 days before
   its 2026-09-03 scan). The other 121/154 DO have some coverage (1-43
   rows), so this second cause is real but partial, not universal — named
   here as a separate, real finding worth its own follow-up (NOT filed to
   PLAN.md per this task's scope — flagged for the operator/session to
   capture); would touch live code, out of scope for a measurement task.

2. `adv_dollar` fed to the scorer was actually the raw SHARE-count `adv`
   column with no `* prev_close` multiplication — mislabeled dollars. Fixed:
   `adv_dollar_0931 = adv_shares_at_tick * prev_close_at_tick`. Did not change
   any prior conclusion (this cohort's real dollar ADV, correctly computed,
   is still ~$0.2M-$10M — nowhere near the $50M liquidity floor either way)
   but it was a real bug in the reconstruction, named here for the record.

3. Every feature (gap, volume, rel_volume) was previously read from EACH
   ticker's own MAX-of-day scan tick — whenever its gap happened to peak that
   morning, a different clock time for every name. For CHPT that was 09:45 ET
   (gap 49.81%), not the 09:31 decision point (gap 34.68%). Fixed: every
   feature for every name now comes from the SAME kind of moment — the
   `mi_ep_scan_log` tick closest in wall-clock time to 09:31:00 ET that day.
   56/154 rows land within 30 seconds of 09:31 exactly; the median distance
   across the full population is 14 minutes, and 30 ticker-days have no tick
   within 30 minutes of 09:31 at all — the scan had already stopped tracking
   that name (its gap faded pre-open) before the 09:31 mark. Used anyway, per
   the brief's "nearest tick" instruction, with the exact distance recorded
   on every row so a reader can judge staleness per name.

   `today_volume` the dedicated column only started being persisted
   2026-08-31 — recovered for the other 141/154 rows by inverting
   `rel_volume = round(today_volume/adv, 2)` (today_volume = rel_volume *
   adv); `market_cap` the dedicated column also only starts 2026-08-31 —
   recovered for the rest by parsing the exact same dollar figure
   `check_filters` already wrote into that morning's `filter_reason` string
   ("$134M < $500M"), falling back to the nearest same-day tick that carried
   the string when the chosen 09:31 tick had failed a DIFFERENT filter first.

Limitations kept (named, not buried):
  - `projected_vol_multiple` is None at the 09:31 tick for nearly every row —
    this is FIDELITY, not a default: live does not compute it before 9:45 ET
    either (CLAUDE.md), so its absence here is what the system would actually
    have known at 09:31. The liquidity fallback path already falls back to
    `rel_volume` when this is None, same as live.
  - `float_shares` is a CURRENT FMP read, not a historical one — same
    simplification the prior study used and named; float is near-static and
    carries no price/outcome information.
  - `catalyst_metrics_extractor` (earnings Q-rev downgrade gate) and the
    holistic judge (`ep_grade_judge`) were not run — same scope decision as
    the prior study; both can only DOWNGRADE or SUPPRESS, so every score
    here is a soft upper bound, never an underestimate.
  - 30/154 ticker-days had their nearest-available tick more than 30 minutes
    from 09:31 (the scan had already dropped the name from its candidate list
    by then, most often because the gap had already faded pre-open) — their
    gap/volume readings are stale relative to the 09:31 label. Flagged per
    row (`tick_dist_from_0931_sec`); not excluded, since a stale-but-present
    reading is still the most honest reconstruction available and the brief
    asks for the nearest tick, not a fabricated one.
  - 5 of the 60 newly-graded ticker-days carry `sec_same_day_risk=True`
    (nearest SEC 8-K/6-K dated the scan day itself, acceptance time missing
    or after 09:31) — same residual caveat class the prior study flagged.

============================== DIRECT ANSWERS ==============================

1. What weighting would admit CHPT?
   Reverting the gap ladder alone (77.5), reverting the full legacy rubric
   (80.0), doubling the volume-conviction weight (65.0, exactly at the bar),
   or rescaling liquidity to this cohort's real ADV$ range (67.5). Honest
   current-rubric volume fix alone does NOT (58.8, 6.2 points short).

2. What does admitting it cost?
   Every one of those variants admits 7 to 32 other names alongside CHPT.
   Their apparent aggregate profit is real-looking (+0.5R to +1.4R/trade) but
   is carried almost entirely by two specific trades (FBRX +10.71R, SENS
   +3.99R) that ride along in EVERY variant regardless of mechanism. Strip
   those two out and every variant's mean return falls to at-or-below the
   cohort's own +0.10R baseline. Both outlier trades' ADMITTING SCORES (not
   their outcomes, which are real) were built from a STALE tick (>5 min from
   09:31) — restricted to the subsample where every score is a clean 09:31
   read, they are excluded by construction and every gap-based admitting
   variant (V1, V2) goes net NEGATIVE on what remains. Admitting CHPT means
   admitting a wide net of mostly break-even-to-losing names whose apparent
   quality is an artifact of two trades whose SCORES were not reliably
   timed, not a real filter.

3. Is there a variant that is positive-expectancy on this cohort AND admits
   CHPT? NO. The one feature that reliably, monotonically, split-half-
   replicates in this data (market cap) argues CHPT belongs in the WORST
   third of this population, not the best. Every mechanism that manages to
   let CHPT in does so by also catching two large, unrelated winners whose
   presence — not any real quality the mechanism detected — is what makes
   the admitted set look profitable. The binding constraint is that this
   cohort's only real signal points away from CHPT, and everything that
   points toward CHPT is noise at this sample size.

============================== OVERFITTING / SAMPLE SIZE ==============================

n=107 settled, several parameters swept — every admitting variant was
checked three ways: chronological split-half (first ~53 vs last ~54 dates,
against EACH half's own baseline, not zero), with its top 1-2 contributing
trades removed, and restricted to the subsample where the point-in-time
reconstruction is actually fresh (within 5 min of 09:31, n=41 — the more
reliable half of the data). Every variant's apparent edge either flips sign,
collapses toward baseline, or both, under at least one of these three
checks — and the two outlier trades (FBRX, SENS) that carry every variant's
headline number are BOTH built from stale, not fresh, ticks. The market-cap
and catalyst-strong-tier findings are the only two that survive every check
(consistent sign across halves, not attributable to a single trade, present
regardless of tick freshness) — and neither one supports admitting CHPT
beyond what the current rubric already credits it. Nothing in this report
should be read as "found a working mechanism" — it should be read as "swept
honestly, and the honest answer is no."

============================== OPERATOR DECISIONS — SURFACED, NOT MADE ==============================
